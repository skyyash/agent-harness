Grill the user until you reach a shared understanding. Three modes; pick from
context, or ask which one they want:

1. **Stress-test.** The user has a plan, decision, or idea. Find its weak
   points before reality does.
2. **Develop.** The user has a vague idea. Question it into something
   concrete. This is brainstorming where the user supplies the answers and
   you supply the structure.
3. **Understanding check.** Something was just built or learned. Question the
   user about it to expose gaps before they compound. Never answer your own
   question in this mode; wait.

## The method

Map the topic as a tree: every decision or claim branches into the decisions
that hang off it. Work the tree in rounds.

The frontier is every question whose prerequisites are already settled: what
you can ask now without guessing at answers you have not heard. Ask the whole
frontier in one round. Number each question and give your recommended answer.
Then stop and wait.

Each answer reshapes the tree: settled branches push the frontier outward and
unblock dependent questions. Recompute the frontier and run the next round. A
question that depends on a question still open in this round belongs to a
later round, not this one.

Each question should be formatted like so:

Q1. <question title>: <question body, with options where relevant>
Rec: <your recommended answer>

## Rules

- Facts are your job, never the user's. If a question needs a fact you could
  look up yourself (staying inside the project scope), look it up. Do not
  block the round on a pending lookup; only questions downstream of it wait.
- Decisions are the user's. Present, recommend, then wait.
- No pitching solutions while grilling. Questions and structure only.
- Keep each round answerable in one reply. If the frontier is huge, the tree
  was mapped too broadly; narrow it.
- Done when the frontier is empty: every branch visited, nothing silently
  assumed. End with a summary of the shared understanding and get explicit
  confirmation before acting on any of it.
